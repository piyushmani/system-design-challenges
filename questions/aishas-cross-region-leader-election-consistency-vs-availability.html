Challenge

Aisha's Cross-Region Leader Election: Consistency vs. Availability

Aisha is designing the control plane of a globally distributed job scheduler that must guarantee exactly-once execution of every job, with a single global serial order of assignments. The platform spans three cloud regions (inter-region round trips of tens to roughly 150 ms). A leader must be elected to assign jobs and commit those assignments to durable state. The team's top priority is strong consistency and correctness: no duplicate execution and no lost acknowledged assignments. They accept higher cross-region write latency and slower recovery during a complete regional failure. The control plane stores small assignment records (job ID, owner, epoch), not job payloads. She must pick the leader-election architecture.

A
Deploy a single Raft/etcd cluster stretched across all three regions (with one voting node located in each region). All client writes are directed to the current cluster leader regardless of its location; the Raft quorum ensures global strong consistency and facilitates automatic cross-region failover.
✓ Best Answer
B
Establish separate, independent Raft clusters within each region (providing local leaders for low-latency regional tasks). Elect a global leader via a secondary election process among the regional leaders using ZooKeeper or a similar coordinator. Rely on this global leader to make globally serialized decisions.
✗ Not Best
C
Operate a specialized Raft cluster (consisting of 3 voting nodes) centralized in one primary region to handle all leader elections and write operations. Asynchronously replicate this state to the other regions, functioning as read-only replicas. In the event the primary region experiences an outage, execute a controlled promotion of the replicas to form a new Raft cluster (through manual intervention or orchestrated failover).
✗ Not Best
D
Implement lease-based leader election utilizing per-region local leaders acquired from a cloud-managed distributed lock or lease service. Allow these local leaders to optimistically assign jobs and reconcile conflicts post-facto (achieving eventual convergence). Favor high availability and low latency, accepting eventual consistency as a trade-off.
✗ Not Best

Answer & In-Depth Explanation

🏆 Best Answer — A: Stretched Raft Cluster (One Voter per Region)

Exactly-once, globally ordered scheduling needs a single linearizable log that survives the loss of any one region without losing acknowledged writes. Only a majority-quorum consensus group spanning the regions provides that: every assignment is committed with 2 of 3 regions before it is acknowledged, a leader is unique per term, and failover is automatic and safe. The price is one inter-region round trip per write, which the team has explicitly accepted. Here's why it stands out:

  • Safety by construction: Raft guarantees at most one leader per term and that committed entries survive any single-region failure. The log index is the global serial order of assignments, so there are no split-brain leaders and no divergent histories.
  • Zero data loss and automatic failover: If the leader's region dies, the other two regions elect a new leader (typically within seconds, governed by the election timeout) with every committed assignment intact (RPO = 0). No human has to decide whether the old region is really dead.
  • Bounded, predictable latency: A commit needs the leader plus one more region to acknowledge, so write latency is about one round trip to the nearest other region. Leader placement can be tuned (priority or leadership transfer) to sit in the region with the most scheduling traffic.
  • Fits the workload: Assignments are tiny metadata writes, so hundreds to low thousands per second are feasible cross-region, and batching or pipelining helps. Job payloads and execution stay regional.
  • Proven building blocks: etcd, Consul and ZooKeeper provide election primitives, leases and revision numbers that can serve as fencing tokens, and they are battle tested (etcd is the control-plane store of Kubernetes).

Run odd-sized voter sets with zone anti-affinity, use the store's election and lease primitives, treat the term/revision as the fencing token, and commit the assignment before dispatching the job. Validate the design with partition and pause tests (isolate each region, freeze the leader, skew clocks) and watch commit latency p99 and leader-change rate.

🔍 Why Not the Other Options?

Each alternative is a valid technique — but none addresses the core problem as directly:

🪜

B — Regional Raft Clusters + Global Election Among Leaders

Electing a “global leader” among regional leaders needs its own consensus service: if ZooKeeper lives in one region it is a single point of failure, and if it is stretched across regions you have rebuilt option A with an extra layer. More importantly, the global leader's decisions are not committed into a global replicated log, so a new global leader has no guarantee of seeing earlier assignments, which produces duplicates and ordering violations. Two nested election layers also create confusing failure modes (a valid global lease while its regional Raft lost its leader). Caveat: hierarchical designs suit regional latency-sensitive tasks that have no global invariant.

🏠

C — Single-Region Raft + Async Replicas + Manual Promotion

Asynchronous replication means replicas may lack the most recent committed assignments. After promotion, those jobs can be re-assigned and executed twice, so acknowledged writes are lost (RPO > 0) and exactly-once is violated. If the primary is merely partitioned rather than dead, a scripted promotion risks two active clusters, and the old primary may return with divergent entries. Clients outside the primary region pay cross-region latency anyway, so there is no latency advantage over A, and recovery takes minutes. Caveat: acceptable only when some data loss is tolerable, or when the replicas are made synchronous, which turns it into A.

🌱

D — Per-Region Lease Leaders with Optimistic Assignment

Allowing several regions to assign jobs at the same time creates duplicates by design and loses the global order. “Reconcile after the fact” cannot undo the side effects of a job that already ran twice. Lease correctness also depends on bounded clock drift between the lock service and the leaders. It is highly available and low latency, but it directly contradicts the stated priority of correctness. Caveat: a good fit for idempotent or commutative work such as cache refreshes, health checks and cron-style jobs where duplicates are harmless.

🎯 When Would You Pick Each?

Every option is the right answer in a specific context:

📌

A — Stretched Raft Cluster

Small, critical control-plane state that needs linearizability, zero data loss on a region failure and automatic failover, and where an inter-region round trip per write is acceptable.

📌

B — Regional Raft + Global Election

Mostly regional workloads with occasional coordination that has no strict global invariant, where regional latency is the priority.

📌

C — Single-Region Raft + Async Replicas

Disaster-recovery setups where some data loss is acceptable and where traffic is concentrated in one region.

📌

D — Per-Region Lease Leaders

Idempotent or commutative work (cache refresh, health checks, periodic cleanup) where duplicates are harmless and availability matters most.

⚠️ Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Assuming election equals exactly-once: A paused or partitioned old leader can still act. Validate a fencing token (term or revision) at every place a decision takes effect.
  • Dispatching before the commit is durable: If the assignment is not committed first, a leader crash can lose or duplicate the job. Commit, then notify.
  • Default timeouts on a WAN: LAN-tuned election timeouts cause constant leader flapping across regions. Set them to several times the RTT plus jitter.
  • Putting payloads in the consensus log: Large values slow every commit. Store only small assignment records, and keep job data in regional storage.
  • Two voters in a two-region deployment: Any single failure removes the quorum. Use three regions (or an odd voter count with careful placement).
  • Not testing partitions: Behavior under asymmetric partitions and frozen processes is where bugs hide. Test them deliberately, ideally with a Jepsen-style harness.

🌍 Real-World Best Practice

Systems that need globally consistent coordination (Kubernetes control planes, Spanner-style databases, distributed schedulers) accept a cross-region quorum cost for the small amount of state that truly needs it. A typical build-out:

  • Step 0 — Write down the invariants: At most one executor per job, what global ordering is required, and what “exactly-once” means (delivery versus effect).
  • Step 1 — Topology: Three voters, one per region (or five in a 2-2-1 layout for extra node-level tolerance), with zone anti-affinity, and non-voting learners in regions that only read.
  • Step 2 — Election and fencing: Use the store's election primitive (etcd campaign with a lease, Consul sessions, ZooKeeper ephemeral nodes) and treat the term or revision as a monotonically increasing fencing token.
  • Step 3 — Commit before dispatch: Record each assignment (job ID, owner, epoch) with a compare-and-set transaction, and only then notify the worker, which includes the epoch in downstream calls.
  • Step 4 — Idempotent execution: A dedupe table keyed by job ID, with the result and the done-marker written in one transaction, or idempotency keys for external calls.
  • Step 5 — Chaos and observability: Partition each region, freeze the leader (SIGSTOP), skew clocks, and verify no duplicate execution while tracking leader changes, commit latency and dropped proposals.

Keep the consensus group small and the payloads smaller, and let regions do the heavy work locally.

💡

Key Takeaway

When correctness outranks latency and availability, use one consensus group across regions. A stretched Raft cluster gives a unique leader per term, a single global log and zero data loss on a region failure. Add fencing tokens and idempotent execution, because leader election by itself does not give exactly-once.
